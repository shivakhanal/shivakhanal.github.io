Excited to share that our paper, "Objective assessment of post-fire canopy recovery
trajectories in south-eastern Australian eucalypt forests," has been published in
*Forest Ecology and Management*.

Using 20+ years of MODIS FPAR time series (2001-2022) combined with S-mode PCA and
k-means clustering, we classified forest phenology types across NSW eucalypt forests
and quantified how quickly canopies recover after fire, comparing prescribed burns
against wildfires across severity classes.

Median recovery time across the landscape was under a year, but trajectories
diverged sharply by fire type. Prescribed burns recovered much faster within the
first two years, while the long tail of slow-recovering areas beyond two years was
similar between fire types.

Grateful for the collaboration with Amila Wickramasinghe, Ivan Kotzur, and Matthias
Boer at the Hawkesbury Institute for the Environment, Western Sydney University.
