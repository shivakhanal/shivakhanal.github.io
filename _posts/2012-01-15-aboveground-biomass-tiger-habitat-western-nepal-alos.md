From April 2011 to March 2012, I worked on a collaborative research project, **"Estimation of Aboveground Forest Biomass in a Tiger Habitat of Western Nepal Using ALOS Data and Field Inventory,"** carried out in partnership with the **Asian Institute of Technology (AIT)** and the **Japan Aerospace Exploration Agency (JAXA)**.

The project set out to map aboveground forest biomass across the Western Tiger Landscape of Nepal by combining multi-temporal L-band **ALOS PALSAR** backscatter (HV polarization) with field-based forest inventory plots. Radar backscatter at L-band is sensitive to forest structure and woody biomass in ways optical imagery often isn't, especially in a landscape with persistent cloud cover during the monsoon, which made it a good fit for building spatially continuous biomass estimates across a tiger habitat that is otherwise difficult to survey comprehensively on the ground.

The resulting biomass model and map of the Western Tiger Landscape (2010) drew on backscatter–biomass relationships calibrated against the field inventory data, producing spatial estimates that could support both conservation planning and, more broadly, Nepal's forest carbon monitoring efforts.

## The paper

The work was written up and presented as a conference paper in January 2012:

> Nashrrullah, S., Khanal, S., Thapa, G. J., & Samarakoon, L. (2012). *Estimation of above ground forest biomass in a tiger habitat of the Western Nepal using ALOS data and field inventory.*

The paper and the resulting [biomass map of the Western Tiger Landscape](https://www.researchgate.net/figure/shows-the-biomass-map-of-Western-Tiger-Landscape-Nepal-in-2010-which-was-derived-from-the_fig2_289259158) are listed on [Gokarna Thapa's ResearchGate profile](https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Gokarna-Thapa).
