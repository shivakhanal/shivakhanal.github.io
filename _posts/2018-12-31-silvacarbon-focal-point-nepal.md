From 2015 to 2018 I served as Nepal's focal point for [SilvaCarbon](https://www.usgs.gov/silvacarbon/), the U.S. Government's interagency technical cooperation programme on forest and terrestrial carbon monitoring, jointly implemented by the [U.S. Forest Service](https://www.fs.usda.gov/about-agency/international-programs) and the [U.S. Geological Survey](https://www.usgs.gov/). The role put me at the meeting point between Nepal's forest monitoring needs and a wide network of international scientists and agencies, and it shaped much of how I approach forest carbon work today.

## National capacity building

A large part of the job was coordination: identifying where Nepal's national forest monitoring system needed support, matching those gaps with the right technical partners, and making sure that training translated into routine work inside government. Over these years that meant workshops, hands-on sessions and follow-up support on satellite data processing, forest change mapping, accuracy assessment and area estimation, and the use of field inventory data alongside remote sensing for REDD+ measurement, reporting and verification (MRV).

## Research initiatives

Alongside training, the programme opened the door to several research collaborations:

- **Tree cover mapping with UMD GLAD.** Work with the [Global Land Analysis and Discovery (GLAD) lab](https://glad.umd.edu/) at the University of Maryland on national tree cover extent and change mapping, a collaboration that began in 2016 and later grew into the [GLAD system implementation project for Nepal](https://glad.umd.edu/projects/implementing-global-land-cover-analysis-and-discovery-glad-system-improved-forest-cover).
- **LiDAR analysis with the U.S. Forest Service.** Analysis of airborne LiDAR data with USFS scientists to improve estimates of forest structure and aboveground biomass.
- **Woods Hole Research Center.** Collaboration with the [Woods Hole Research Center](https://www.woodwellclimate.org/) (now Woodwell Climate Research Center) on forest biomass and carbon mapping.
- **FAO.** Technical exchange with the [Food and Agriculture Organization](https://www.fao.org/forestry/en/) on national forest monitoring tools and methods.
- **World Bank.** Engagement linked to the World Bank's [Forest Carbon Partnership Facility](https://www.forestcarbonpartnership.org/) and Nepal's REDD+ readiness and emission reductions programme.
- **GFOI.** Participation in the [Global Forest Observations Initiative](https://www.reddcompass.org/), including the [GFOI Capacity Building Summit](https://servir.icimod.org/events/gfoi-capacity-building-summit-supporting-efforts-to-build-national-forest-monitoring-systems/) hosted at ICIMOD in Kathmandu in September 2017, which brought together countries and partners from Asia, Africa and the Americas to align support for national forest monitoring systems.

## Looking back

Being the focal point taught me that capacity building works best when it is tied to real national products. The tree cover maps, LiDAR biomass models and MRV discussions from this period fed into Nepal's later forest reference level and land cover monitoring work, and the relationships built with GLAD, USFS and others have continued well beyond the life of the role.
