In November 2016 I represented Nepal at the [Regional Orientation on Long-Term Environmental and Socio-Ecological Monitoring](https://www.icimod.org/event/regional-orientation-on-long-term-environmental-and-socio-ecological-monitoring/), hosted in Pokhara by ICIMOD's Kangchenjunga Landscape Conservation and Development Initiative (KLCDI) together with the Institute of Forestry, Tribhuvan University, with support from GIZ and ADA. Over five days, 48 participants from four transboundary landscape initiatives spanning Bhutan, China, India, and Nepal compared notes on long-term environmental and socio-ecological monitoring (LTESM), worked toward standardized monitoring protocols for priority ecosystems, and drafted country-specific action plans.

My contribution, included as Annex 10 of the workshop report, was **"Monitoring forest resources of Nepal using permanent sample plots established for forest resource assessment."** I walked through Nepal's national permanent sample plot (PSP) network, the backbone of the country's periodic forest resource assessments, re-measured over time to track growth, stocking, and forest change, and discussed how that long-term plot-based approach fits into the kind of standardized, comparable regional monitoring the workshop was pushing toward across the Hindu Kush Himalaya.

The full [workshop report](https://lib.icimod.org/records/4r76n-96j35) is published by ICIMOD (2017); my presentation is Annex 10.
