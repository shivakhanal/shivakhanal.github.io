This week (12 to 16 October 2026) we are hosting a [South Asian regional workshop](https://sites.google.com/view/wilpa-nepal-workshop/home) in Kathmandu on forest removals, fire and biomass monitoring. The Ministry of Agriculture, Forest and Environment (MoAFE) and the Forest Research and Training Center (FRTC) are hosting it, with the programme delivered together with [Wilpa Capacity Development](https://www.wilpacd.com/).

The workshop brings together forest monitoring and GHG inventory teams from Nepal, Bhutan, Pakistan and Lao PDR, alongside experts from the European Commission's Joint Research Centre, FAO, ART TREES, Google, the University of Oviedo and Wilpa. With colleagues from FRTC, the Department of Forests and Soil Conservation, ICIMOD, Tribhuvan University and FAO Nepal, there are 34 [participants](https://sites.google.com/view/wilpa-nepal-workshop/participants) in the room.

The common thread is a practical one: how countries in the region can move from IPCC default values toward nationally grounded estimates of forest removals and fire emissions that stand up in GHG inventories, Biennial Transparency Reports and results-based payment programmes.

**What the five days cover**

- **[Day 1](https://sites.google.com/view/wilpa-nepal-workshop/day-1): Removals in national reporting.** How forest removals feed national monitoring, GHG inventories and results-based reporting, followed by country presentations from Nepal, Bhutan, Lao PDR and Pakistan on current methods, data and institutional roles.
- **[Day 2](https://sites.google.com/view/wilpa-nepal-workshop/day-2): Reporting standards and fire emissions.** Removals reporting under ART TREES Version 3.0 and ongoing FAO work, my talk on estimating forest fire emissions from Nepal's national forest inventory plots, and an introduction to Google Earth Engine.
- **[Day 3](https://sites.google.com/view/wilpa-nepal-workshop/day-3): From pixels to plots.** Hands-on Earth Engine exercises, then measuring biomass and fire effects on the ground (pre-fire condition, severity, biomass loss, tree survival, dead wood and recovery), drafting a field protocol, and a country discussion on strengthening national emission and removal factors.
- **[Field day](https://sites.google.com/view/wilpa-nepal-workshop/field-day)** to put the protocol into practice, and a closing day on [Day 5](https://sites.google.com/view/wilpa-nepal-workshop/day-5).

All presentations and training materials are shared openly on the [workshop website](https://sites.google.com/view/wilpa-nepal-workshop/home).

For me, hosting this was a chance to put two parts of my work in the same room: the transparency reporting we do at the Ministry, and the field and remote sensing research that should inform it. Countries in this region face many of the same data gaps, and comparing notes on how each of us is filling them has been the most useful part of the week. My thanks to the FRTC team, the Wilpa team, and every resource person and participant who travelled to Kathmandu.
