Presented on [Day 2](https://sites.google.com/view/wilpa-nepal-workshop/day-2) of the [South Asian regional workshop on forest removals, fire and biomass monitoring](https://sites.google.com/view/wilpa-nepal-workshop/home) (12 to 16 October 2026), hosted in Kathmandu by MoAFE and FRTC with Wilpa Capacity Development, with participants from Nepal, Bhutan, Pakistan and Lao PDR.

Nepal currently reports forest fire emissions with IPCC Tier 1 defaults for fuel load and combustion factor. The talk asked whether the national Forest Resource Assessment (FRA) plot network can supply Nepal-specific values instead. I combined 1,622 FRA forest plots measured between 2010 and 2014 (fire signs and intensity, litter and debris, dead wood, seedlings and 15 other disturbance types) with MODIS burned area for 2001 to 2025, and used mixed-effects models, paired burned and unburned plots, and a Monte Carlo uncertainty analysis.

**Key findings**

- **Fuel load:** burned plots carry only slightly less litter and fine debris than unburned plots (about 0.09 t/ha less, interval including zero). Litter varies more between forest types and regions than with fire.
- **Combustion factor:** most recorded fires are undated and litter refills within a year or two, so paired plots cannot yield a usable national value. The few pairs where MODIS dates the fire to within a year of the visit give 0.31, the same order as the IPCC default of 0.45. This supports keeping the default rather than replacing it.
- **Emissions:** a hybrid estimate (FRA fuel loads × IPCC combustion and emission factors × MODIS burned area) gives about 1.1 t CO2e per hectare burned and around 0.18 Mt CO2e per year nationally. The Siwalik and Terai hold 41% of the forest but account for 83% of the burned area and about 79% of emissions: fire frequency, not fuel load, sets where emissions arise.
- **A lower bound:** the estimate covers litter and fine debris only, and MODIS misses many small surface fires. Including dead wood could raise per-hectare emissions six- to ninefold, which makes dead-wood consumption the largest single unknown.
- **Regeneration:** burned plots have about 20% more seedlings, consistent with a regime of moderate surface fires.

**Implications for Nepal's BTR:** keep the IPCC default combustion factor, use FRA fuel loads by stratum, report the result as a lower bound with its uncertainty interval, and use the inventory's fire records as ground truth for satellite fire maps and as an indicator of forest resilience. A true Tier 2 combustion factor will need dated fires or plots measured before and after burning, and the dated pairs show that such a design would work.

This is ongoing work with the FRA plot data; the results are preliminary.
