Tests whether the extreme aboveground biomass density recorded in Central Himalayan high-elevation forests, exceeding 1000 t/ha, is an artefact of the allometric equations, wood density values, or species composition used to estimate it. Using national forest inventory data, finds that high-biomass-density plots persist irrespective of the specific allometric model or wood density values applied, pointing to forest structure, rather than methodology or species mix, as the primary driver of the exceptional carbon density observed.

Collaboration with Rachael H. Nolan, Belinda E. Medlyn, and Matthias M. Boer at the Hawkesbury Institute for the Environment, Western Sydney University.
