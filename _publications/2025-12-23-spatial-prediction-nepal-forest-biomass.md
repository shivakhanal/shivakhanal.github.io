Introduces a two-step, data-driven approach to mapping Nepal's forest aboveground biomass (AGB). A clustering step first groups locations by tree species and topoclimatic variables to predict potential AGB; a machine-learning step then models deviations from that potential using proxies for forest structure, disturbance likelihood, and elevation zone. The approach cuts AGB prediction error by 25% relative to the best-performing existing model, yielding a 30 m resolution national AGB map (total: 1982 Mt) and a 250 m potential-AGB map with prediction uncertainty. Confirms the Central Himalayas as a key carbon reservoir, with forest patches exceeding 1000 t/ha, and supports climate mitigation, restoration monitoring, and REDD+ tracking.

Collaboration with Rachael H. Nolan, Belinda E. Medlyn, and Matthias M. Boer at the Hawkesbury Institute for the Environment, Western Sydney University.
