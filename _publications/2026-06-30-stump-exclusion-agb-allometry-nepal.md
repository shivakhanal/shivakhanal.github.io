Nepal's Forest Research and Training Centre (FRTC) national allometric equations model aboveground biomass (AGB) excluding the basal 30-cm stump. Using the publicly available FRTC destructive-sampling database of 476 trees from seven commercially important species, this study reconstructs ground-level AGB and finds that the stump accounts for a mean of 3.61% (range 1.34% to 11.5%), with its share declining strongly with tree diameter overall and within every species. Because the effect is size-dependent, it cannot be handled by a single correction factor. Restoring the stump explained about one fifth of the gap between pantropical (Chave) predictions and measured biomass, so biomass boundaries should be documented and harmonised before estimates are compared.

I contributed to this study, led by Santosh Ayer at the University of Alberta. It builds on openly published data from Nepal's forest biometry work, and is one example of how making such data openly available supports follow-up research on other aspects of forest biometry.
